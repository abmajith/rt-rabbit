Bus Handshake & Read/Write Protocols
    Zero-Wait-State Acknowledge: 
    The core asserts wb_ack_o exactly $1$ clock cycle following a valid cycle qualification:
        $$\text{bus\_write\_en} = \text{wb\_cyc\_i} \cdot \text{wb\_stb\_i} \cdot \text{wb\_we\_i} \cdot \overline{\text{wb\_ack\_o}}$$
        $$\text{bus\_read\_en} = \text{wb\_cyc\_i} \cdot \text{wb\_stb\_i} \cdot \overline{\text{wb\_we\_i}} \cdot \overline{\text{wb\_ack\_o}}$$

    Bus Deadlock Prevention: 
    Because wb_ack_o clears unconditionally on the clock cycle following an assertion 
    (unless a brand new transaction qualifies on the exact same edge), the peripheral cannot stall or hang the system crossbar interconnect.

    Default Slicing Safety: 
    If unmapped addresses (2'b11) are requested during a Read command, 
    the data bus safely squashes output leakage by returning 32'd0 explicitly.

Defensive Hardware Saturation Engine
The core features a built-in protection system inside its comparator module to completely prevent clock 
wrap-around glitches or short-pulse hazards when parameters cross over during runtime:

    Absolute $0\%$ Clamp State: When reg_duty == 32'd0, the output bit pwm_pad_o is completely latched down to 1'b0. 
    This holds true even if the internal counter rolls over or resets, eliminating partial or transient pulses at zero speed.
    
    Absolute $100\%$ Clamp State: When reg_duty >= reg_period, the logic forces an absolute override, locking pwm_pad_o to a continuous 1'b1. 
    This guarantees clean DC saturation without dropping the line low for a single clock cycle at max speed.
    
    Period Boundary Alignment: The pulse counter wraps exactly at the count boundary:$$\text{Max Count} = \text{reg\_period} - 1$$
    This ensures the wave duration matches your expected mathematical frequency precisely, without adding an extra trailing clock cycle overhead.
